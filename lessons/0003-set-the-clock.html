Lesson 3: Set the Clock

Reverse the skill — build the map from digital to spatial.

Reading a watch is half the skill. The other half is the reverse: seeing a digital time and knowing where the hands go. When you can do both directions fluently, the spatial map becomes truly automatic — you stop translating and just know.1

How It Works

You'll see a digital time. Drag the hands on the clock to set it. Use the buttons to switch between moving the hour and minute hand, then hit Submit.

Think in anchors. When you see "7:45", don't plot 45 individual minutes. Think: "minute hand points left (the 9 anchor), hour hand is just before the top-right (almost at 8)." Translate to shapes, not numbers.

Why This Helps

Reading a clock is recognition — you see hands and decode them. Setting a clock is production — you imagine where hands should go. Production is harder, but it builds stronger spatial memory.2 Once you can produce the hand positions from a digital time without hesitation, reading them back becomes effortless.
